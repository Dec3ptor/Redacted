Your photo knows more than you do.

Every picture your phone takes carries a hidden record: the exact coordinates where you stood, the second you pressed the shutter, the serial number of the camera, and often a small copy of the original image from before you edited it. Drop a photo below to read all of it, then decide line by line what gets deleted.

Drop a photo here JPEG files carry the most metadata — or tap to choose

The file is read by this page, not by a server. Nothing is uploaded, stored, or transmitted. If a website offers to strip your metadata but asks you to upload the photo first, it has your photo and its metadata.

What is worth removing, and what is harmless

Location is the one that matters. GPS coordinates are precise to a few metres. A photo of a sofa posted to a marketplace listing can carry the address of the living room it sits in. If you remove nothing else, remove this.

Timestamps place you somewhere. Capture time is often more revealing than location, because it establishes that a specific person was in a specific place at a specific moment.

Serial numbers link your photos together. Body and lens serial numbers are unique to your hardware. Two photos posted under two different names, carrying the same camera serial, are provably the same photographer.

The embedded thumbnail is the quiet one. Cameras store a small preview alongside the full image. Some editing software updates the main image but leaves the old thumbnail in place — which means a cropped or retouched photo can still contain a miniature of what it looked like before. This has exposed people who thought they had cropped someone out of frame.

Exposure settings, focal length, and colour profile are generally harmless and are worth keeping if you care about the photo. That is the reason this page lets you choose rather than just deleting everything.

If your document is a PDF or a scan rather than a photo, the redaction tool destroys metadata as a side effect of flattening the page.
